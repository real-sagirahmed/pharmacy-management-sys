Challenges Faced by Pharmacies in Bangladesh  
Before understanding the solution, let’s look at the real problems pharmacy owners face every day.

1\. Manual Billing Errors  
Many pharmacies still use calculators or handwritten cash memos. In a rush, mistakes happen—wrong pricing, missed discounts, or incorrect quantity. These small errors reduce profit and damage customer trust.

2\. Expiry Losses and Wastage  
Medicines have strict expiry dates. Without proper tracking, shops often discover expired items only after it’s too late. This leads to financial loss and even risk of mistakenly selling unsafe products.

3\. Stockouts and Dead Stock  
One common problem is running out of fast-moving medicines while keeping shelves full of slow-moving brands. Without a clear view of demand trends, owners often tie up cash in dead stock while disappointing customers with frequent “nai” answers.

4\. Supplier and Payment Management  
A typical pharmacy in Bangladesh deals with multiple suppliers. Each supplier has different terms, trade offers, and credit periods. Without a proper ledger, it’s hard to track dues, returns, and bonuses. Disputes are common.

5\. Compliance and Audit Risks  
The Directorate General of Drug Administration (DGDA) requires proper records of purchase, sales, and batch details. During audits, manual registers often create confusion. A missing invoice or incorrect batch record can lead to penalties.

6\. Customer Experience Gaps  
Customers today expect quick billing, digital payments like bKash or Nagad, and even prescription record-keeping for future visits. Pharmacies that can’t meet these expectations lose out to modern competitors.

These are the pain points pharmacy management software is designed to solve.

Common Mistakes to Avoid in Pharmacy Digitalization  
Switching to software can be life-changing for a pharmacy. But many owners make mistakes that reduce the system’s value. Here are the most common pitfalls in Bangladesh and how to avoid them.

1\. Going Live Without a Clean Item Master  
If your medicine catalog has spelling mistakes, duplicate entries, or missing batch details, billing will become messy.

Solution: Prepare a clean item master list (brand, generic, pack, strength, MRP, supplier) before rollout.

2\. Skipping Batch and Expiry at Purchase  
Some staff enter purchases without batch and expiry details, thinking it saves time.

Problem: Expiry alerts won’t work, and stock tracking will fail.

Solution: Make batch entry mandatory during purchase.

3\. Giving All Staff Admin Rights  
If every staff can edit purchase prices or give discounts, you will lose money.

Solution: Use user roles. Cashiers only bill; managers approve exceptions.

4\. Not Testing Backups  
Many pharmacies take backups but never test restores. During a real crisis, they discover backups were corrupt.

Solution: Test restore once every month.

5\. Ignoring Training for New Staff  
Old staff may know the system, but new joiners often get no training.

Solution: Have a simple training manual or short video for newcomers.

6\. Mixing Personal Expenses in Pharmacy Accounts  
Owners sometimes record personal expenses in daily sales.

Solution: Keep pharmacy accounts separate for clear profit/loss.

KPIs Every Pharmacy Should Track  
Software gives a lot of reports, but the challenge is knowing what to focus on. Here are the most important Key Performance Indicators (KPIs) for a Bangladeshi pharmacy.

1\. Stockout Rate  
Definition: How often you run out of fast-moving items.

Why track: Customers leave if you frequently say “nai.”

2\. Expiry Loss Percentage  
Definition: Value of expired stock as a percentage of total sales.

Target: Keep under 1%.

3\. Gross Margin by Category  
Definition: Profit earned after purchase cost.

Why track: Some categories (like antibiotics) have higher margins than generics.

4\. Dead Stock Value  
Definition: Value of items not sold in the last 90 days.

Why track: Dead stock locks up cash that could buy fast-moving items.

5\. Billing Speed  
Definition: Average time per bill.

Why track: Slow checkout reduces customer satisfaction.

6\. Discount Leakage  
Definition: Total discounts given vs. approved.

Why track: Prevents unauthorized discounts.

7\. Cash Variance  
Definition: Difference between system closing balance and physical cash.

Why track: Exposes theft or mismanagement.

8\. Repeat Customer Rate  
Definition: % of customers who come back within 30 days.

Why track: Repeat customers are more valuable than one-time buyers.

Set uniform MRP and discount rules from the head office. Branches should not randomly give discounts.

Branch managers should have limited rights. All sensitive operations (like supplier payments, new item creation, and price changes) should be controlled by head office.
